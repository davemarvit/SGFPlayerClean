Offline turn identity and location experiment — stage 12
2026-11-17

DECISION
Keep the prototype offline. It demonstrates that a certified turn can retain its
identity, color and original clock boundary while several legal intersections
remain possible. Sustained recording evidence improves location resolution in
conditional chronological replays, without another turn or changed timestamp.
It does not yet justify changing live recognition, clock policy or capture handling.

ARCHITECTURE ASSESSMENT
The requested separation already partly exists in the app:
- CandidateMoveHistory derives candidate-specific episodes and timing witnesses
  from retained observations. Its bounded episode identity includes location
  alternatives, so it is not a permanent identity for a played turn.
- ClearBoardTiming and GameRecorder certify turns independently of legal location
  search; ProvisionalPlay separately checks retained paths and capture agreement.
- LocalGameClock keeps provisional presses and promotes them into saved moves
  without awarding increments again. Existing timing uncertainty, expiry, missing
  turns, camera continuity and capture-pause checks remain authoritative.
- MoveObservationHistory retains legal candidate sequences and permits later play
  and corrections to resolve them. It has bounded search/retention, whole-position
  verification, placement concerns and persistence requirements.
- DeferredMoveHistory preserves chronology when a gap needs explicit review.

The smallest useful new experiment is therefore a position resolver beside an
immutable turn ledger, not another clock engine or a replacement pixel detector.
No app source, build registry, installed app, running app, saved game, recording,
calibration or clock sidecar was changed by this work. Existing working-tree edits
were preserved. Live source hashes match those taken before the native regressions.

IMPLEMENTATION
Tools/turn_location_lab.py contains an offline-only model with three independent
parts: certified turn evidence, a forest of legal positions, and physical stones
awaiting removal. Turn identity excludes location. A reliable color, identity,
ordered timing interval and distinct witnesses are required at the input boundary.
These are supplied certifications, not new measurements from pixels. A location
correction only filters retained paths. Duplicate delivery is idempotent; reused
witnesses or a changed timestamp for the same identity require timing review.

Legal boards are advanced using group liberties, opponent capture, suicide
rejection and simple ko. This small rules implementation supports Japanese-style
no-suicide placements only; it is not a replacement for SGFPlayer or all supported
rulesets, passes, handicap setup and superko. Initial board context must be trusted.
Unreliable surrounding readings never overwrite that context. Ambiguous earlier
moves supply alternative legal boards rather than guessed surrounding stones.

The resolver can eliminate a location when subsequent certified play is illegal
there, or when repeated reliable local observations contradict its legal board.
It requires three distinct observation IDs over at least 0.8 seconds, with no
inter-observation gap above 0.75 seconds. Hand obstruction, unknown hand state,
lighting failure and unreliable points interrupt the corresponding evidence runs.
A declared camera gap latches timing review and preserves already certified turns.
No missing placement time or increment is invented. Sparse recording samples also
reset persistence; they are not themselves proof that the live camera stopped.

Legal prisoners are tracked by their original stone identity. They may remain
physically visible for an arbitrarily long removal delay. Visibility alone cannot
disprove a capture. Repeated clear emptiness can establish removal and distinguish
positions. Sustained surviving stones can reject a predicted capture only with a
separately certified local removal-completion context. The prototype does not
derive that context from a hand exit or timeout; the recorded replays do not supply
it. This is a deliberate remaining limitation.

Occupied evidence that no retained position yet explains is retained as unassigned
evidence for the upstream turn detector. It cannot create a press or delete every
position while a later turn certificate is still arriving. If all legal positions
conflict with other sustained evidence, the previous forest is preserved for review.

Unresolved locations are exposed by turn identity. The position budget is 256;
overflow retains the entire previous forest and the complete turn ledger, without
silently retaining only the highest-scored paths. Further position advancement and
automatic correction are then unavailable. Location-only uncertainty recommends
continued play. With automatic multi-stone capture pausing enabled, disagreeing
capture plans require capture review and agreed incomplete captures require a hold.
These are policy recommendations, not live clock commands or inferred pause times.

EXPERIMENTS AND RESULTS
Tools/evaluate_turn_location.py uses 1,385 chronological observations from the
65-move recording and 1,658 from the 85-move recording. It retains their existing
native readings, timestamps, hand flags and outlines. Saved turns and clock
boundaries are supplied, not rediscovered. Adjacent alternative intersections are
injected stress cases. The observations used to distinguish paths require heuristic
confidence >= 0.98; occupied points also need outline quality >= 0.90 and center
offset <= 0.24. These gates are not calibrated confidence probabilities or proof
that historical placement warnings can be cleared.

Each move is tested with its true saved location and legal adjacent alternatives,
then all subsequent recorded turns and observations are processed chronologically.
Two of the 150 moves have no legal adjacent alternative, leaving 148 injected
ambiguities. The same two recordings are reused across all controls; these are
correlated development probes, not 450 independent games or an unseen holdout.

                          65-move recording      85-move recording
Initially ambiguous       64                    84
Legality alone resolved   10                    6
Sustained evidence        61                    77
Assignment delayed 5 s    61                    76

Counts above exclude the two initially unique controls. Sustained evidence resolves
138/148 ambiguities; 10 remain marked. Delaying assignment evidence five seconds
resolves 137/148. All three policies retain the supplied true path, produce no wrong
resolution, accept every supplied turn and preserve every ledger timestamp. All
150 retained-location corrections per policy preserve the ledger exactly. The two
recorded single-stone captures are reproduced: game65 move56 removes point172;
game85 move35 removes point288. This does not validate removal-pause timing.

The known game85 move56 at point33 remains unresolved. Nothing overrides its
centering warning. Sixteen stage-11 fixture/point controls, including halfway and
crowded placements, cannot certify turns from static evidence and remain untimed.
These static controls test the input contract, not new discrimination of the images.

A harder experiment injects alternatives at EVERY turn in each game. The forest
overflows at turn7 of game65 and turn5 of game85, retaining 97 and 125 prior paths
respectively. All 65/85 turn identities and original times still survive, but the
position resolver cannot complete or accept batch correction. These stress runs
explicitly disable automatic capture pauses; their continue recommendation must
not be generalized to uncertain multi-stone captures with pausing enabled. With
that policy enabled an unknown capture plan requires capture review. This result
identifies a substantial scaling limit, not a successful full-game decoder.

One development failure was informative: an opponent stone became visible before
its recorded turn certificate arrived (game85 frame1300, near the move35 capture).
The first draft treated it as a contradiction of every existing path. The final
version preserves such globally unsupported occupied evidence as unassigned until
turn certification. It does not infer a move from the observation. A focused test
and the repeated chronological replay cover this change. Earlier draft outputs
are retained for investigation; replay-final is the reproducible final result.

CLOCK AND FAILURE CHECKS
The journals provide 114 actual native candidate certifications (50 and 64), each
with two distinct original observation IDs. Isolated ambiguity/correction probes
preserve those exact press times. All 684 injected missing-witness, hand, gap,
unreliable-color, unreliable-identity and unknown-time controls abstain. These are
fault injections into supplied certifications, not a new hand or identity detector.

Tools/TurnLocationClockAudit.swift links only the unchanged LocalClockEngine. It
reproduces all 150 saved before/after clock checkpoints with zero differences.
Duplicate delivery and correction leave accounting identical in all five timed
modes. The saved full clock payloads remain unchanged. Saved clock anchors lacking
an estimate are labeled as such; physical timing uncertainty is not invented.

21 focused Python tests pass, including delayed and partial prisoner removal,
capture disagreement/hold/completion, ko, suicide, multiple unresolved turns,
atomic batch correction, hand/lighting interruption, camera gaps, duplicate image
IDs, duplicate presses, overlapping times, false absence and bounded-search overflow.

The existing recognition_benchmark.py ran seven build-registry checks and 18 native
suites. Registry checks and 17 suites pass, including candidate histories, rapid
reply/near-overtime replay, provisional play, placement timing, hand clocks,
historical correction, capture uncertainty and automatic capture timing. Rapid
reply retains zero timestamp/balance differences and no false holds in its supported
cases; its missing-evidence control still requires review.

workflow-ui-test fails reproducibly in the existing ResumeVerification scenario:
the move advances from 172 to 173 with no pending move and an unchanged historical
board, but the test expecting a running White clock fails. It fails both in the
shared-runtime suite and when rebuilt separately. This prototype is not linked into
either test; the unchanged native failure is retained as a promotion blocker.
No production fix or weakened assertion was made in this offline task.
An isolated copy of the scenario confirms that White is selected correctly, while
needsCorrection is true and the clock reports: "Move timing crosses a clock hold
or has no retained turn boundary. Check both times before resuming." The fixture
constructs resumed views without camera timestamps. This distinguishes a timing
review from a wrong-player switch; it does not establish whether the test expectation
is stale or the resume path needs repair. ResumeClockProbe.swift and
resume-clock-probe.json retain the offline reproduction and state.

CAMERA BOUNDARIES
The recent camera is the AUSDOM AF660. Older recordings made with a zoomed iPhone
11 Pro Max remain a separate calibration family. The two full chronological replays
use their own saved camera observations and setup metadata (camera ID
0x21420000bda0567); no lens, focal-length, grid or parallax calibration is fitted or
transferred. Static historical fixtures retain their original camera provenance.
This experiment does not claim a full chronological iPhone validation.
