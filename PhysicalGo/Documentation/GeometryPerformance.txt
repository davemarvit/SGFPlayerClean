Recognition processing efficiency — 2026-11-19, stage 19

WHY THIS WORK
Existing diagnostic recordings show that the sparse-board AF660 incident usually
completed analysis in about 70 ms, below its 200 ms sampling interval. Earlier,
fuller-board recordings often took about 170–220 ms, with slower observations
above 300 ms. These are recorded operation durations, not new live measurements.
A native sample identified outline fitting as a substantial computation cost.

The earlier AF660 clock stop remains a separate evidence problem. White move 4
and Black move 5 were both visible by the first globally cleared observation.
Intermediate observations were blocked by uncertain or overlapping hands. They
do not establish two verified clock boundaries. This optimization does not use
those blocked observations to invent clock presses or remove that safeguard.

CHANGE
Every candidate outline sums horizontal spans from a row-prefix buffer. The
disk shapes and buffer stride are fixed, but the old inner loop recalculated
each span's row offset for every candidate center. Each span now stores its
two linear offsets once, when the immutable disk shapes are initialized.

The old addresses were (centerY+spanY)*stride+centerX+right+1 and
(centerY+spanY)*stride+centerX+left. The new addresses are centerY*stride+centerX
plus the corresponding precomputed offsets. They address the same samples in
the same order, preserving every floating-point sum, radius, score, threshold,
tie-breaking order and search range. Arrays remain bounds checked; no unsafe
buffer access, cross-image cache, new mutable state or approximate arithmetic
was introduced. Recognition and clock policies are unchanged.

COMPARISON
Tools/GeometryPerformanceAudit.swift runs the actual optimized native detector.
Image decoding and result serialization are outside the measured interval. One
warmup precedes each image's timed repetitions. Each repetition must return the
same complete recognition evidence: all intersection labels/confidences/deltas,
stone outlines, placement fits, centering evidence, lighting field and warnings.

Tools/test_geometry_performance.py compiles the previous StoneGeometry.swift
from the specified Git revision and the candidate with the same remaining
sources and compiler. It compares complete outputs on every case, then measures
17 selected cases in baseline/candidate/candidate/baseline order, with three
timed repetitions per case per trial. The aggregate is the sum of per-case
median detector times, averaged across the two trials of each version. A
measured improvement of at least 5% and zero evidence differences are required.
Inputs and source hashes must remain unchanged throughout the run.

The corpus has 560 image/calibration cases: the previous 495 geometry cases,
12 empty-point/capture controls, four older iPhone observations, and 49 recent
AF660 observations including hands, rapid replies and the white-stone incident.
There are 390 distinct image files, including previously generated lens-test
images. These are not 560 independent games or all unmodified camera images.
Each observation retains its own recorded calibration and reference. Testing
previously generated grid/lens variants does not enable those experiments.

Scope: this tests deterministic recognition and computation time. It is not a
new accuracy improvement, a calibrated probability estimate, a demonstration
that all clock stops disappear, or a live end-to-end latency measurement.

RELEASE GATE
validate_deferred_release.py accepts --geometry-comparison only when the report
passes and its source/input hashes still match. It adds photographed faint
capture, shaded-white, jade-white and hand/stone pipeline checks to the existing
16 native suites, then checks disabled-feature compatibility, both complete
recorded games, the AF660 white-turn incident, identity handling and the signed
app build. The comparison file and build-source hashes are checked again before
the candidate can be installed. The previous installed app remains available
for rollback. Image collection stays off; saved games and unrelated edits are
preserved. The installed app is not opened automatically.
The complete-game clock tests replay the original chronological observations;
the raw-image comparison covers selected images, not every image in both games.

MEASURED RESULT
Zero recognition-evidence differences across all 560 cases. The summed per-case
median time fell from 0.8552 seconds to 0.6743 seconds, a 21.1% reduction. Both
candidate trials beat both baseline trials (baseline 0.8516/0.8587 seconds;
candidate 0.6781/0.6706 seconds). The 85-move endpoint fell from 135.7 to 93.5 ms;
the 65-move endpoint fell from 113.8 to 85.3 ms. The two occupied recent AF660
cases improved by about 14–15%, and the four iPhone cases by about 15–21%.
The empty recent AF660 case was about 0.3 ms slower (1.8%); there is little
outline-search work to save on an empty board. These numbers measure the
detector on this Mac, not image decoding, camera delivery or total app latency.

REGRESSION RESULTS
The frozen source passed 56 connected identity/persistence checks, 27 white-turn
incident checks, all 20 enabled-feature native suites, all four disabled-feature
compatibility suites, and 32 Python identity tests. Both complete recorded move
sequences were preserved. The 85-move replay retained all 85 measured press times
unchanged; the 65-move replay retained its original first-turn timing review.
The photographed hand/stone pipeline passed, as did faint-capture, shaded-white,
jade-white and crowded-white controls. No missing clock boundary was filled in.
