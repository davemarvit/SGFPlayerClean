85-move game: startup timing diagnosis and expanded recognition evidence
2026-11-16

Decision

Keep the existing Start/countdown behavior. No first-move-free policy was
implemented, for either an even or handicap game. No live app code, clock
records, capture settings, or installed application changed in this work.
The startup accounting defect below is reproduced, NOT repaired.

The whole captured game now has an offline observation/candidate inventory,
an 85-move timing ledger, six additional reviewed negative point samples,
and a development experiment on stone-aware background sampling. The original
frozen unavailable-shadow validation remains a failure; this later experiment
does not retroactively turn that failure into a validation pass.

Startup: two related problems

The automatic pause lasted 6.722 seconds. Camera images continued arriving.
At frame 190, the operation lasted 0.8747 seconds, but its recorder entry was
only 0.0506 seconds after operation start. The remaining 0.8241 seconds occurred
between recorder entry and operation completion. That interval includes the
recorder callback, notifications, sound callbacks and diagnostic overhead;
it is not an isolated vision inference measurement. The Start callback invokes
NSSound("Pop") synchronously, but the logs do not isolate its cost. Profiling is
needed before blaming sound, disk writes, or a particular subroutine.

ready() started the clock at uptime 1326263.0938285 and advanced the camera
freshness marker to that time. The next delivered frame was captured slightly
earlier, at 1326263.0515002501, and was ignored for freshness. When frame 193's
fresh observation arrived at 1326264.6754035417, the old freshness marker was
1.582 seconds old, beyond the 1.5-second guard. noteCameraObservation checks
that gap before installing the new observation, so the clock held. The guard
protects unseen thinking time and must not simply be disabled or lengthened.

The hold also cleared the uninterrupted-turn baseline. Move 1 was subsequently
accepted while stopped; it switched the player without charging Black. Resume
created a new White baseline after White's estimated physical press. Moves 2
and 3 then had observed times earlier than their respective retained baselines.
recordMove silently used recognition time instead of those estimates, without
requiring clock review. The multi-move catch-up path is stricter; this sequence
arrived as separately accepted single moves and bypassed that protection.

StartupClockReplay.swift reproduces this call path with the recorded uptime
values using the real LocalGameClock implementation. It reproduces the exact
saved balances after move 3: Black 117.1146611667, White 115.4340048335, with
estimates 1, 2 and 3 unapplied and requiresReview false. Healthy-continuity
controls still charge the first turn for either Black or White. This is a
diagnostic reproduction, not a regression claiming the defect is fixed, and
not an end-to-end camera/recorder playback.

White's first hand-clear-to-hand-clear interval is approximately 3.002 seconds
(observed bracket endpoint range 2.788–3.216), whereas the recorded deduction
is 4.566 seconds. Black's first turn is zero. These are evidence of allocation
failure, not permission to rewrite saved times or a verified reconstruction
of all thinking time across the hold.

Full-game timing coverage

Of 85 saved move records:
  71 apply their stored estimate exactly at the engine boundary.
   2 have estimates that were not applied (moves 2 and 3).
   1 has an estimate but no running anchor (move 1).
  11 use a candidate-time fallback without a stored hand-clear estimate:
     4, 5, 11, 17, 18, 21, 40, 65, 70, 75 and 84.

The eleven do have engine boundaries; they are not eleven completely untimed
moves. They require separate confidence/uncertainty treatment. For example,
move 4 shares move 3's engine boundary and therefore receives zero elapsed
time. The fallback originates in appendVerifiedSequence's first retained
candidate timestamp. A populated anchor is not proof of correct physical time.

Stored estimates comprise 67 hand brackets, three hand-motion estimates and
four clear-frame estimates. Among moves with the hand-clear timing scope,
median recognition delay is 0.855 seconds, p90 1.854 seconds, maximum 5.262.
Delay until recognition is distinct from the timestamp used for charging.
No additional automatic pause occurred after startup. Eight long-hand warnings
were logged while the clock continued running.

Expanded image evidence

Inventoried all 1,658 analyzed frames from 2,669 saved images. The 1,011 skipped
analysis opportunities still have saved images. 910 analyzed frames have an
explicit clear hand flag, delivered outcome, and no lighting/exposure block.
The inventory preserves all capture times, operation times, uncertainty points,
raw stone candidates and saved-position comparisons. Saved-position mismatches
remain proposals, not labels: a genuine next move often precedes SGF acceptance.

Eight strong clear-view candidate episodes at locations never present in the
record were visually checked. All eight are empty intersections on the right
camera edge, across FOUR hand-entry events:
  frame 341:  raw points 56, 94;       hand detected 0.391 seconds later
  frame 605:  raw point 189;          hand detected 0.186 seconds later
  frame 978:  raw points 227,246,265;  hand detected 0.390 seconds later
  frame 1981: raw points 246,265;      hand detected 0.205 seconds later

Frame 1981 was already reviewed; the other six point samples are new. Each
episode has just one strong clear observation. None became a saved phantom.
Five of the eight still receive strong support from the existing appearance
weighting, despite the visible absence of stones. All eight have native Black
confidence at least 0.9; seven are 1.0. Higher raw confidence alone cannot
separate these errors from real stones.

This supports the proposed pre-hand-shadow mechanism. It does not prove a hand
will always be detected, or that no hand means no move. Any retrospective
discount must become available only when the later hand evidence arrives,
preserve the original candidate timestamp, and leave missed-hand/camera-gap
cases unknown where evidence is insufficient.

Current ShadowEvidence lookback is already 0.8 seconds, but its eligibility
requires uncertain/weak readings or sufficiently broad clusters. These events
escape those assumptions: the one/two-point false readings are very confident;
the three-point event has zero uncertain intersections. Merely lengthening the
lookback would not fix that selection problem. Temporal persistence and legal
move constraints are useful here; this is not evidence to replace them with a
single-frame classifier.

Stone-aware background sampling experiment

The previous feature extractor excluded circles centered on ideal intersections
from its wood sample. An off-center stone can extend beyond those circles and
contaminate the supposed background. The development ablation additionally
excludes current-frame fitted ellipses (quality >=0.8, radius 0.25–0.75 grid
intervals, maximum axis offset 0.5, padding 0.08). One variant also centers the
core sample on the fitted stone. No thresholds were optimized on this game.

At Black 13, frame 476 / raw point 23:
  75 of 216 original background pixels are excluded by the fitted outlines.
  Background residual falls from 0.34282 to 0.01196.
  Center darkening remains approximately 0.42467.
  The previously rejected real stone regains strong support.

At Black 21 from the earlier 65-move game:
  Background residual falls from 0.20270 to 0.01750.
  The true placement remains supported.

With the unchanged contrast policy thresholds, the outline-mask variant
supports all 85/85 reviewed placements, versus 84/85 with the original geometry,
and preserves 65/65 from the earlier game. Moving the core adds no placement
coverage benefit in these samples, so masking alone is the simpler candidate.
The old 65-game false edge sample remains rejected. The two original 85-game
false edge samples remain supported. Across all eight expanded edge negatives,
five remain strongly supported, unchanged by either geometry variant.

These are selected point/frame checks, NOT a validated full-game decoder or
calibrated probability model. Outline-dependent sampling can itself trust a
bad outline; older difficult fixtures, temporal windows, missed-hand faults,
camera gaps and capture sequences still need evaluation before promotion.
Both recent games are development/regression data for any subsequent tuning.
An untouched game will eventually be needed for final validation, not yet.

Safeguards and next action

All 55 offline Python tests pass. The native diagnostic reproduces the bug
and passes its ordinary first-turn controls. All 2,679 preservation-manifest
entries still match their original sizes and SHA256 hashes. The frozen policy,
protocol and placement model hashes are unchanged. The single captured-stone
removal at move 35 and its reviewed before/after images remain intact.

Priority: repair clock accounting across an automatic hold while retaining
Start/countdown semantics. Retain the relevant timing segments and explicit
uncertainty; apply delayed presses only when continuity is supported. A press
that cannot be assigned safely must not silently become recognition time.
Test manual pauses, genuine processing gaps, capture removal, byoyomi and
expiry-boundary cases before installing that change. Separately profile the
startup callback; reducing its stall would prevent some holds but would not
repair the underlying accounting path.

Recognition: extend the outline-mask ablation through temporal replay and
older regressions, then test whether pending high-confidence edge candidates
benefit from later hand-entry evidence without adding blanket move latency.
Do not erase candidate histories or use future evidence as if it existed at
the original observation time. No additional game is needed for this work.
