Continuing play while cleanup locations remain uncertain
Offline implementation and deployment assessment, 2026-11-17

DECISION
Implement the native state and matching boundary now, and keep it disconnected
from live recognition. Deployment does not require knowing every stone's exact
intersection. It does require accepting each reliably identified turn exactly
once, preserving its original time through correction/restart, and preventing a
location hypothesis from becoming a new move, a capture, or a clock instruction.

The new native implementation satisfies those boundaries in the tests below.
It is included by the normal app source glob but has no live caller. No existing
recognition, recorder, clock, capture or calibration source was edited. No app
installation or relaunch was performed. A compiled inactive implementation is
not evidence that changing the live recorder's decisions is ready.

WHAT WAS IMPLEMENTED FOR DEPLOYMENT
Sources/DeferredStoneState.swift contains a typed immutable turn certificate and
a separate mutable location record. A turn can have several candidate points or
no known location at all. Location work cannot change its identity, color, order,
original time or clock press. Duplicate delivery is a no-op; changed evidence
under an existing identity is rejected. Widespread ambiguity uses independent
candidate sets per stone rather than a tree of complete games.

A current physical center is separate from the historical intersection that will
eventually appear in SGF. Background proposals update the proposed display center
and retain alternatives. They do not settle historical locations. A reviewed
intersection can be supplied later without another move or clock change. Final
move export refuses unresolved history. The offline exporter then uses the app's
existing SGF parser and rules engine to check every resulting board and capture.

Background proposals carry a state revision and process epoch. Accepting another
turn, changing capture state, reviewing a location or invalidating a view makes
old proposals unusable. Hand reentry explicitly clears fresh display support and
invalidates in-flight work. Restoring a checkpoint retains alternatives and review
decisions but clears claims of fresh visibility and gives workers a new epoch.

DeferredTurnSession exercises transactional ledger/clock persistence using the
unchanged LocalClockEngine: save one atomic replacement file, then publish the
new in-memory state. Failed writes commit neither a turn nor a clock transition.
Restoration replays certificates to verify the saved clock. Redelivery after
restart cannot press twice. Monotonic clock origins must match; an arbitrary new
process or reboot cannot reuse an old timing basis. This adapter is offline; it
must not become a second clock beside the app's LocalGameClock. Atomic replacement
is tested, not a claim of power-loss durability or multi-writer locking.

Sources/DeferredCleanupAssociation.swift follows outlines across a hand visit.
It considers moves up to 2.25 grid spacings and retains every plausible local
association. A fresh outline must have one eligible old identity, and that old
identity must have one eligible outline. The nearest same-color stone does not
win a tie. Even a unique match remains a proposed association, not proof of a
physical object's identity while hidden.

The competition includes unreliable neighboring stones, newly placed same-color
stones, and captured stones awaiting removal. An identity with unknown whereabouts
can block a match without supplying a guessed position. A captured identity cannot
revive; it remains a possible physical explanation until removal is independently
verified. Removing one competitor permits fresh observations to support the
survivor, but disappearance alone does not eliminate an identity.

Anchors require sustained pre-hand evidence. A post-hand association requires at
least three distinct clear observations over 0.8 seconds, within 0.12 spacings of
the first post-hand center. Hand coverage/unknown hand state, lighting failure,
camera/setup changes, missing observations and coherent board movement withdraw
fresh support. Work limits defer location work only; they do not stop turn intake.
The search radius and outline-quality values are heuristics, not calibrated
probabilities. Association evidence has no authority to determine legal captures.

global board-position budget or changed the next reliably supplied turn.

LARGER MOVEMENTS AND SAME-COLOR CONTROLS
Four previously visually reviewed hand-covered small adjustments supply original
outline shapes and observation timing. Each is deliberately translated by one
and two grid spacings, with the remaining scene removed to create an isolated
identity control. All eight translated controls support a proposed association.
Adding a competing same-color explanation makes all eight remain unresolved.
These are modified observations, not eight real large-cleanup recordings.

Native unit controls additionally exercise equal-distance alternatives, unequal-
distance alternatives, same-color swaps, two identities explaining one outline,
a missing neighbor, unknown whereabouts, a newly placed same-color stone, delayed
capture removal, verified removal and fresh survivor reassessment. No identity is
merged because its outline disappeared. A clock turn never originates from these
association results.

The actual RepositionedPending fixture contributes 41 original observations of
the known relocation/double-display failure. Its missing hand history is kept
unknown, so all 41 observations abstain from establishing continuity. A supplied
hypothetical anchor in this negative control is explicitly not a recorded pre-hand
observation. The existing native repositioning regression independently checks
the known surviving sequence. Separate clips are not stitched into a trajectory.

VERIFICATION
The new standalone native executable passed 54 unit assertions and the combined
replay passed 27,005 assertions. Most are repeated invariants across observations
and clock modes, not independent test cases. The two reviewed minimal SGF exports
match every intermediate board and captured-stone total through all 150 moves in
the existing native rules engine. Original game metadata and sidecars are untouched.

The 32 existing Python identity/occlusion tests passed. All six targeted application
regression suites passed: observation replay, provisional play, capture/clock,
established stone tracking, repositioning and crowded-white adjustment. The normal
app build and signing also passed. Outcomes are recorded alongside this report in
validation.json; their logs are retained. A first regression compilation overlapped an edit to a new file and
was rejected by Swift's changed-input check; the affected suite is rerun after
the source is frozen. That attempt is retained and is not counted as a passing run.

Tests/DeferredRecorderReplayTests.swift closes part of the supplied-turn testing
gap: it feeds the existing RapidReplyShadow recording observations into the actual
unchanged GameRecorder, then consumes the newly produced native turn decisions in
the new ledger. No saved acceptance decisions are supplied. The three actual
recognized turns retain their independently expected times, with every new-ledger
location unresolved, zero review transitions and the clock running. The state is
serialized/restored after each observation without changing the native clock.
Removing two intermediate sightings leaves only one timed turn and preserves the
native timing review. That is missing turn evidence, not a location-only pause.
This remains an offline shadow test: uncertain locations do not feed back into
native recognition. It does not yet prove that replacing GameRecorder's pending
position model would preserve its new-turn decisions during larger cleanups.

WHAT REMAINS BEFORE LIVE ENABLEMENT
This work removes the need for exhaustive history trees or perfect cleanup
identification as release conditions. Unresolved cleanup locations are acceptable
in a deployed system. The remaining integration work is specific:

1. Connect the existing turn/timing authority to the immutable ledger and the
   proposed display, with one owner for clock events. Keep that connection inactive
   until full recorder replay shows that cleanup cannot create a turn or conceal
   a real new placement. The two full-game tests here supply known turn certificates;
   the additional three-turn recorder test exercises actual recognition into a
   shadow ledger, without changing the recorder's pending-position model.
2. Integrate the unresolved physical-position markers and post-game review/export
   with GameRecorder's existing save/restore format. The new checkpoint format has
   no migration from current pending/review sidecars. This implementation handles
   alternating placement turns on an initially empty board; pass, handicap/setup,
   undo, edited turn order and existing-game migration need explicit adapters and
   tests before replacing the current recording path.
3. Keep native capture legality and automatic capture timing authoritative, and
   prove capture-start/removal/resume behavior with the connected recorder. The
   new location state validates supplied capture identities and delayed removal;
   it does not infer capture legality or decide when the existing capture clocks
   should pause. Its isolated session adapter cannot backdate a press across a
   timing hold or restore a missing timing boundary.

These are implementation/integration requirements, not a request for the user to
resolve every ambiguity or make another recording. The current recordings and
native fixtures remain useful for the next connected offline tests. The earlier
ResumeVerification test's timing-review behavior is not repaired by this work;
location uncertainty must not be used as a reason to bypass genuinely missing
turn-time evidence.
