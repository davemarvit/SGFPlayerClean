Why Black 13 fails

At frame 476, raw point 23 (col 4, row 1), the real stone is off-center. The native
outline quality is 0.9968, with fitted center (4.12,0.72): its row offset of
0.28 exceeds the outline fallback's frozen 0.20 limit. The measured center
darkening is 0.4228, background residual 0.3428, and contrast margin only 0.0800,
below the frozen 0.14 requirement. Native support is 0.650364, just above 0.65.

The evidence suggests fixed grid-centered sampling confounds off-center stone
appearance with background disturbance. This is a diagnosis to test, not proof
that simply widening a threshold is safe. No threshold was relaxed to pass
the held-out example.

A follow-up diagnostic examined original frames 449–518 without retuning. The
native control has 19 strongly supported clear observations from frames 471
through 518 over 9.797 seconds; the proposed policy has none. This is not merely one
unlucky snapshot. The diagnostic retains all original timestamps and makes no
clock-press assignments or automatic gap recovery claims.

Why the two false readings escape

At frame 1981, raw points 246 and 265 (rightmost camera column, rows 12 and 13)
are visually empty. Both native Black readings have confidence 1.0. Their
background errors are low, 0.0362 and 0.0290, and the appearance calculation
marks them available. Their signed core changes are -0.0989 and -0.1350, enough
for the existing shadow weighting to remain above the support threshold.

The new rule only handles UNAVAILABLE appearance; it leaves these existing
available-but-misleading estimates untouched. Confidence 1.0 plus an apparently
valid background metric still does not establish a real stone. The live game's
other decision safeguards prevented these samples becoming saved moves.

Clock audit: separate issue

There was one automatic pause, lasting 6.722 seconds, immediately after Start
(saved move count 0) and recovering at count 1. Its reason was "Board processing
fell behind. Checking a fresh view before continuing." No later automatic
pauses and no clock-correction flags appear. Eight prolonged-hand warnings
occurred during the game, with the clock running in their logged states.

The camera continued delivering images. Around startup, operation 190 took
0.875 seconds and operation 191 finished with a frame age of 1.473 seconds. The
existing observation-gap protection stops at the last verified observation
when fresh processing falls behind; recovery also waits for board reconciliation.
These records support a processing/backlog interruption, not a six-second
absence of camera images. They do not identify the resource-contention cause.

Move 1's stored clock before/after values both leave Black at 120 seconds. Thus
the startup hold did not charge its elapsed turn time. This requires a separate
clock-accounting review; absence of a correction flag is not proof of accuracy.
The frozen offline recognizer has no live clock authority and did not cause or
repair this behavior. No stored times were changed.
