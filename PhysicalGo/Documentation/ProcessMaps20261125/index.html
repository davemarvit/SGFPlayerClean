Five architectural findings worth studying

The current bottlenecks are different problems: recognition repeatedly rejects ambiguous evidence; aiming spends time collecting new evidence and changing hardware state; temporal tracking can reject a good hold. One broad “camera is slow” explanation would send optimization to the wrong place.

1. Measure proof collection and state transitions separately from image computation

High confidence in the distinction; speedup unmeasured. The earlier Q17 retry was already at its target, accepted with zero moves, yet crossed two PREPARE/SWEEP cycles. approach() starts with observe(), whose default darkens after measurement. hold() then prepares and illuminates again because it receives a dark operation. This is an implementation boundary worth profiling. The same installed host can already promote an accepted later lit landing into holding without another light command.

Potential simplification: investigate retaining the first accepted beam through the transition to monitored holding, only if the same operation, pose, geometry, remaining illumination budget and fresh target evidence remain valid. The fresh holding check is still independent proof; deleting it would change the contract. This proposal could remove a dark/prepare cycle for near-target starts, but it is not part of the diagrammed baseline; no physical speedup is established. S15: approach / observe / hold.

2. Treat recognition delay as an evidence-state problem before changing cadence

High confidence in the observed cause; no recognition fix established. PG23 R16 took 25.418 seconds across 255 fresh frames, with 130 ambiguity resets. Capture-to-consume age was median 122 ms, maximum 151 ms; AI answered about 0.495 s after board confirmation. A strong Black stone was blocked by a weak expected-empty neighbor. Increasing frame rate alone may produce more resets.

Potential simplification: make the one blocking assignment visible in diagnostics and retain its exact outline/grid checkpoint. Investigate expected counts by color as a consistency constraint, explicitly accounting for pending AI stones, captures awaiting removal and corrections. Counts cannot locate a stone or prove hidden emptiness. Any change must preserve real halfway-placement rejection. S10: consume 534–620 · S1: PG16 / PG23.

3. Keep independent proofs, but make their scope and invalidation explicit

High confidence in the boundaries; medium confidence in consolidation benefits. Native grid/hand checks, host dark registration, fixed-anchor temporal checks, fresh spot measurement, actual motor pose and firmware limits answer different questions. A matching file hash cannot prove an unmoved pointer; a fresh safety message cannot substitute for fresh optical pixels; correct aiming cannot confirm a stone. These are necessary independent checks.

Potential simplification: one documented evidence record should identify which camera epoch, frame set, setup, motor pose, target revision and deadline each proof covers. Cache only compatible geometry and make the reason for renewal explicit. Today several state transitions and cache policies express overlapping identity checks in separate modules. Consolidating their description and diagnostics first can expose redundant work without collapsing separate safety responsibilities. PG35 demonstrates the consequence of scope confusion: a session-age timer had been used for a later admission; the installed repair scopes the unchanged 12 s deadline to an unfinished admission episode. S11 · S13 · S16 · PG35.

4. Separate appearance, geometry and coverage failures in one first-fault record

High confidence that they differ; temporal repair remains under review. Live brightness scores are diagnostic-only. Installed dark registration uses ECC to propose a transform, then independent features and distributed grid tracking to admit geometry. The latest failure was later temporal reuse: roughly one-grid-pitch jumps in 13 tracks drove p95 to 53.709 px despite median 0.069 px and a consistent majority.

Potential simplification: report “appearance changed,” “motion estimate failed,” “spatial coverage inadequate” and “geometry moved” as distinct evidence outcomes. Retain the first exact rejected batch and track distribution; avoid treating a pooled percentile as a complete physical explanation. The reviewed and installed normalization repair normalizes exposure for tracking while preserving the original pyramid and coverage rules: exact frame 21221 gives 147/160 valid tracks, median 0.077 px and p95 0.207 px; 22 controls and the legacy covered-quadrant rejection passed. This supersedes the shallower-pyramid experiment. The final owner-reported 30-repeat offline timing adds about 0.61–0.84 ms: exact-frame full reuse median 3.84→4.68 ms (p95 4.34→5.29 ms), and historical occupied-frame median 3.43→4.04 ms (p95 3.74→4.62 ms). This is a reliability repair, not a compute speedup. Exposure repair 5de5479 passed 112 focused tests and paired acceptance, then its attended run failed a separate scheduled dark-registration check. The subsequently installed 968ba538d457c03fc34ed0b17b0d59e853817838-app0264 passed its bounded dark-validator checks but the final attended retry again failed temporal reuse at frame57014: median 0.062 px, p95 27.330 px. Neither repair establishes sustained physical reliability or total-latency improvement. The diagrams and source index retain the fc8cb7d process-flow baseline. Later normalization and dark-validation changes do not change that high-level flow; their algorithmic details are outside the pinned source snapshot. S16 · S17 · S18 · PG06a / PG21.

5. Use one correlated turn timeline, with separate sound and optical milestones

High confidence in missing measurements; instrumentation proposal only. Native once-per-move click occurs on the first acquisition-light acknowledgement, potentially many seconds before final target acceptance. The host final cue is separate. PG26b does not establish audio onset or Bluetooth latency. PG41 lit fine corrections are installed but the zero-move retry did not exercise them.

Potential simplification: correlate human placement witness → recognition acceptance → AI answer → host dispatch → first-light acknowledgement → optical target acceptance → verified hold → physical confirmation. Record active computation, new-frame wait, serial/settling wait, mandatory flush and rejected-evidence retry time separately. Keep acknowledgement timestamps distinct from actual visible/audible onset. This would reveal whether the largest gain comes from recognition quality, fewer state transitions or less repeated work. Preserve Mac monotonic versus wall time, and never subtract MCU elapsed time from Mac uptime. PG21 / PG26b / PG41 · S5 · S15 · S27.
