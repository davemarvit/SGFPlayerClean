Continuing play while locations remain uncertain — offline experiment
2026-11-17

DECISION AND PURPOSE
The main requirement is continuity: uncertain stone locations must not stop the
clocks or prevent another reliably timed turn from being recorded. Locations can
be checked during play and resolved after the game. This experiment supersedes
the stage-12 choice to expand all combinations of ambiguous move locations.
It does not replace live recognition or change the running application.

The new model keeps one identity per stone, a separate immutable record of turns,
and evidence for each candidate intersection. Five alternatives for each of 150
stones mean 750 individual entries, not combinations of 150 alternative histories.
The provisional display chooses a location but retains uncertainty markers. If
two identities prefer the same intersection, that conflict remains explicit;
neither identity is silently discarded or relocated to make the display fit.

CONFIDENCE AND CLEANUP
Tools/stone_identity_lab.py separates:
- Whether a turn, its color and its timing have been certified upstream.
- Whether the association with the same physical stone is still reliable.
- Evidence for each possible intersection, including contradictory observations.
- Current visibility and verified capture/removal status.

Location scores summarize up to eight recent reliable observations, on a scale
from -1 to +1. They are evidence scores, NOT probabilities or percentages. A
location needs at least three consecutive supporting views over 0.8 seconds and
a score of at least 0.8 to receive the supported label. Repeated delivery of one
observation cannot manufacture persistence. The recording adapter retains the
earlier confidence/outline gates; none of the pixel classifier thresholds changed.

A hand, unknown hand state, bad lighting or a recording-observation gap interrupts
the local evidence run. A verified camera outage remains an independent timing
problem: it cannot be repaired by pretending that missing turns had known times.
Location uncertainty, conflicting guesses, missing stones and an ambiguous movement
association do not themselves request a clock pause in this model.

When a tracked stone moves, its location evidence is reassessed. With independently
supported identity continuity, fresh observations can change its provisional
location without changing its move number, color, original timestamp or clock
credit. Previous evidence is retained in the motion audit. Without identity
continuity, disappearance/reappearance remains an association for later review;
it is not automatically called either a cleanup or a new move. The prototype
accepts motion/identity evidence through an explicit input contract; it does not
yet detect physical movement or certify identity continuity from pixels itself.

This distinction matters when two neighboring stones have the same color, or when
a stone was captured and a later stone appears nearby. Competing identities prevent
one visible stone from supplying strong location support for both identities.
Observed location support is not proof of the player's original intended move:
final historical locations still undergo explicit legal replay after resolution.

BACKGROUND WORK
Reassessment runs in bounded slices with fair scheduling across stones. Results
are stamped with the stone's evidence revision and the shared context revision.
A result prepared before another observation, motion or new turn is rejected as
stale. The background interface has no clock command and cannot edit the turn
ledger. The experiment simulates this scheduling serially; no background worker
was installed in the app and no UI responsiveness claim is made.

CAPTURES AND THE FINAL GAME RECORD
A provisional guessed board never authorizes capture removal or a clock pause.
The model accepts verified capture identities from the existing rules/capture
authority. Those stones remain physically tracked while removal is delayed.
Unreliable disappearance alone is not a capture. Confirmed removal does not create
a new turn. If the earlier location remains uncertain, its historical alternatives
remain available even after the stone is removed.

At the end, a complete explicit mapping from move identity to intersection is
replayed once. Occupancy, liberties, captures, suicide and simple ko are checked.
Illegal or incomplete resolutions are rejected atomically; timestamps are never
rewritten. The offline exporter produces a minimal Japanese-rules move SGF for
verification, not a replacement for the application's metadata/clock-aware writer.
Passes and other rulesets are outside this small prototype. Known gaps in turn
continuity prevent claiming that the exported record is a complete game.

RECORDED-GAME RESULTS
The 65- and 85-move recordings supply the original turn identities, order, clock
boundaries and verified capture context. This is a conditional location experiment,
not a new end-to-end turn detector. It uses the original chronological observations
and four controls per game:

1. Only the saved location supplied.
2. Alternatives injected at EVERY move, with background reassessment.
3. Alternatives at every move and ALL location evidence withheld to the end.
4. Alternatives at every move, with only four stones reassessed per observation.

Every control accepts all 150 original turns across the two recordings, with zero
location-induced timing reviews and identical original timestamps. Even control 3
retains every turn while every move location stays unresolved. Candidate storage
reaches 324 entries for game65 and 425 for game85, with no combination search or
position-budget overflow. All background queues finish processing.

With alternatives at every move and regular background reassessment:
  game65: 27 supported locations, 38 still marked uncertain;
  game85: 25 supported locations, 60 still marked uncertain.
No supported location contradicts the supplied saved record. These figures are
conservative development results, not calibrated accuracy. The provisional display
still guesses 20 and 39 locations incorrectly respectively; those guesses are
explicitly uncertain and cannot authorize captures or clock changes. Withholding
all location evidence deliberately leaves all provisional guesses wrong in these
test inputs, without losing turn timing or preventing final correction.

Restricting background work to four stones per observation yields 26 and 25
supported locations, while retaining every turn. The repeated controls share the
same two recordings; they are not eight independent games or an unseen holdout.

Final resolution uses the supplied saved locations as simulated user corrections.
Tools/StoneIdentitySGFAudit.swift uses the app's actual SGF parser and Go rules
engine to check every resulting move, intermediate board and capture. All eight
exports agree with their original games. Tools/TurnLocationClockAudit.swift checks
the unchanged clock engine: all saved checkpoints match, and duplicate delivery
or correction gives no additional clock credit in all five timed modes.

The actual RepositionedPending fixture contributes 41 chronological observations.
It lacks explicit hand-clearance metadata and a certified placement time for this
test. The new adapter therefore retains one supplied existing identity, creates
zero timed moves, and leaves its location unresolved. This is an abstention control,
not a claim that real cleanup was automatically recognized. Cleanup reassignment
is currently verified in controlled tests with supplied identity continuity.

VERIFICATION AND BOUNDARIES
17 focused tests pass: all-turn ambiguity, location scores, cleanup, uncertain
movement, same-color identity competition, stale background proposals, fair
scheduling, hand/lighting interruption, camera gaps, duplicate observations,
delayed/partial capture removal, atomic final correction and incomplete-game export.

No app source or installed/running app changed. Original recordings, calibration,
SGF files, clock sidecars and unrelated working-tree edits are preserved. Source
and input hashes are recorded. Existing stage-12 native regression results remain
separate: 17/18 suites passed, with the ResumeVerification test expecting clock
restart while the native timing safeguard requests review. That test was not
weakened, and no live timing safeguard was changed by this experiment.

The AUSDOM AF660 and older zoomed iPhone 11 Pro Max calibration families remain
separate. The replays use each recording's existing observations and setup; no
calibration, lens correction or geometric model is transferred between cameras.
