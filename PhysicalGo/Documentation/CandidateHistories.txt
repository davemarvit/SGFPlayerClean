Candidate histories — stage 2

Baseline: feca90c (stage 1 implementation c7b0b5e), version 0.188.
Implementation branch: codex/physical-go-candidate-histories.

CandidateMoveHistory derives one color/location hypothesis from the bounded
ClearBoardTiming observations. It is now used for local turn confirmation,
pending-turn timing protection and provisional placement timing. Each query uses
its own candidate's witnesses; there is no shared first-appearance time for a turn.
Full legal-board and capture-completion checks retain their separate requirements.
Clock arithmetic, recognition thresholds and the SGF legal-history resolver are
unchanged in this stage.

A history records:
- The raw first appearance and its immutable observation ID.
- The first qualifying sighting and its observation ID, independently of raw
  appearance. Shadow suspicion cannot rewrite the original readings/scores.
- The last definite absence and contradiction; absence or an opposite color
  starts a new appearance episode. Uncertainty alone does not mean empty.
- An appearance interval with an optional lower bound. Missing absence evidence
  remains missing; no physical placement time is invented.
- A supported hand-exit estimate, retaining its interval and method, separately
  from the first clear image and the chosen clock estimate.
- The fresh confirmation witness's capture and processing times. This is the
  witness certifying the current decision, not a claim about the earliest possible
  certification. The durable press records it once without changing placement time.
- Possible locations, current observed location and observed occupancy separately.
  A single visible location does not certify which alias the SGF should use.

Identity and retention
The episode identity includes the observation epoch, color, possible locations and
first retained raw sighting. It survives quality revisions while that sighting is
retained. It changes on definite removal/reappearance and epoch reset. These are
bounded current hypotheses, not permanent game move IDs: histories share the
existing 120-second / 1,000-view timing cache, and consumed or expired evidence is
not resurrected. Existing pending-game persistence still governs reopening, which
requires fresh confirmation. Candidate summaries are diagnostic, not authorization
to reuse a clock press after restart.

Occupancy, location, order and timing
An observed stone can have confirmation evidence while its order relative to an
opponent's stone remains unresolved. relativeOrder requires repeated sightings
and intervening repeated empty evidence; first-detection sorting is insufficient.
This relation is advisory. The existing legal-history resolver and clock catch-up
checks continue to decide whether an order can be committed and timed. A later
candidate retains its own evidence even when an earlier pair's order is unclear.

Journal provenance
Observed and provisional clock presses journal candidate summaries. Verified SGF
sequences also include per-candidate summaries alongside their completed-board
estimates. During a provisional capture, appliedPressTime identifies the actual
capture-clock boundary separately from the candidate's placement estimate. The
candidate summary is evidence, not an alternative clock ledger.

Hand shadows
All immediate and retrospective shadow suspicions are retained as quality metadata.
Retrospective timing retractions are tracked separately from immediate filtered
readings, preserving stage-1 timing semantics. Shadow clearance remains explicitly
unknown; a precise hand exit never certifies that a hand's shadow has cleared.
The separate shadow-clearance experiment remains in ObservationReplay.txt.

Verification
Run:
python3 PhysicalGo/Tools/recognition_benchmark.py --output /absolute/new/report-folder

The benchmark now runs 17 suites plus the seven build checks. CandidateHistoryTests
compares the production decisions against StageOneTimingOracle, a frozen test-only
copy of ClearBoardTiming from feca90c. It checks turn identity, timing estimates,
confidence levels/counts/spans, pending timing and provisional/completed-board
estimates. Inputs include twelve constructed scenarios and the 75-operation
recorded rapid reply (59 delivered observations). The oracle receives the same
interpretations and lifecycle events but calculates its decisions independently.
The complete recorder replay separately checks actual saved moves, false pauses,
clock balances and the missing-evidence control. Other suites guard captured
photographs, capture completion, camera continuity, review/resume and phantom moves.

This stage is a behavior-preserving consolidation with explicit provenance and
uncertainty. It does not claim a new pixel classifier or measured physical timing
accuracy. Frozen-oracle equivalence is supplemented by independent scenario
expectations, clock reference schedules and capture regression checks.

Validation completed 2026-11-16
All 17 suites and all 7 build-registry checks passed. The differential comparison
made 3,608 decisions across 299 frames, with zero differences from stage 1.
There are 23 focused history assertions plus the aggregate differential assertion.
The full recorder replay preserved the three moves and their separate supported
press estimates, with zero false holds and zero clock-balance differences in all
four configurations, including the captured near-overtime balances. Removing the
intermediate sightings still required a clock review. Capture checks included 429
automatic-capture/hand-clear assertions, recorded faint-capture photographs and
capture uncertainty/conflict cases. Camera, review/resume and orientation passed.

The signed 0.189 / build 190 app was built and its source hash verified against
the tested sources. CandidateHistoriesResults.json retains the metrics and hashes.
The installed/running application and the user's game were not replaced or relaunched.
