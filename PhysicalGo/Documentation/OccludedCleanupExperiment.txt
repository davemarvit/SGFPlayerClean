RESULT
The new offline association code supports retaining the original stone identity
in all four visually reviewed small adjustments where a hand covers the target.
The targets belong to moves 29 and 59 of game65 and moves 1 and 8 of game85.
New stones are placed during those same hand visits; one visit also includes a
capture. The code waits for fresh clear outlines before updating a proposed center.
It neither creates another turn nor changes the original turn time.

The principal rule remains continuity: uncertainty about this association is
deferred location work. It does not command a clock pause or prevent accepting
another independently reliable turn. No live recognition or installed app changed.

IMPLEMENTATION
Tools/occluded_identity_lab.py adds a small independent association component.
An anchor describes an already-known stone identity and its last stable outline,
supported by at least three distinct pre-hand observations over 0.4 seconds.
The actual replay uses four pre-hand clear observations and rejects unstable or
duplicated anchors. It does not obtain an anchor's color from the saved game when
the recording itself has an unknown color.

During a recorded hand visit, the identity is retained but no association is
confirmed. Afterward, each eligible fresh outline must have exactly one plausible
old identity, and that identity must have exactly one plausible outline. Matching
uses color, fractional center and outline radii. It does not select the nearest
stone when more than one could fit. Duplicate fits of the same physical outline
are merged before matching, without merging distinct nearby stones.

The maximum supported center change is 0.65 grid spacings. Radius changes larger
than 0.12 do not match. The post-hand outline must remain within 0.12 of its first
clear center for at least three distinct observations over 0.8 seconds. Comparing
against the first center prevents repeated small changes from accumulating into
unbounded drift. Existing native outline quality/color gates are used; there is
no new hard centering cutoff that discards a strong displaced outline.

These thresholds define this experiment's conservative support rule. They are not
probabilities. A supported result remains an association hypothesis: a camera
cannot prove which identical stone is under a fully covering hand. The result
explicitly says physicalIdentityProven=false and clockAuthority=false. It is never
converted silently into certified identity continuity or a final historical edit.

Native verified capture identities exclude captured stones from reassociation.
Thus a same-color outline appearing where a captured stone used to be cannot
revive that old identity. Delayed removal and game clock decisions remain with
the existing capture logic. A gap longer than 0.75 seconds, changed camera/setup,
or a coherent displacement of several independent anchors stops this association
attempt and retains the identities unresolved. A hand reentry or unavailable
view withdraws a previously fresh result; old evidence cannot masquerade as fresh.

A gap in the saved observations is missing association evidence, not automatically
proof that the live camera stopped. This component requests no clock action. The
separate turn/timing authority must still handle genuinely unobserved moves; it
cannot invent their times merely to avoid a pause.

The 3,003 stone/visit anchors include the same stones across many visits; they are
not 3,003 independent stones. There are no supported associations conflicting with
the supplied saved intersection and none supported during a recorded hand-covered
observation. This agreement is conditional on saved identities and capture context;
it is not independent ground truth for physical object identity.

All four reviewed hand-covered adjustments are supported. Fresh support takes
approximately 0.80–0.99 seconds from the first qualifying post-hand observation.
This is background verification delay, not a new placement timestamp or a charge
to the player who adjusted the stone. Two of the three nearby-placement controls
are also associated; the other lacks a stable pre-hand anchor and remains unresolved.

The 2,078 still-current association updates are interleaved with the actual saved
turn-certification events. Proposed centers are journaled beside existing identities;
no new stone identity is created. Every original turn number, color and timestamp
remains unchanged. Clock checks through the actual LocalClockEngine reproduce all
150 saved checkpoints with zero differences, including duplicate-delivery checks
in five timed modes. These checks preserve existing timing; they do not establish
new physical placement-time accuracy.

For every eligible hand visit, three independent fault controls are tested:
missing evidence during the hand visit; unknown hand state; and all anchored
identities declared retired to simulate capture/replacement exclusion. All 330
controls abstain from fresh reassociation. These injected faults apply to the
association channel, not to the supplied turn ledger. They do not claim that play
could safely continue if the timing authority itself lost the turns.
