Offline placement competition experiment — 2026-11-16

Decision
Keep all new scoring offline. The color-specific shadow control is a promising
development result: it avoids selecting the captured B19 phantom, preserves all
three real placement locations and Black's timing witnesses, and loses no photo
probes relative to the raw-confidence experimental control. Competition itself
has not shown additional benefit over independently scoring the same candidates.
This is not yet a validated improvement to the live recognizer or clock system.

Implementation
Tools/placement_competition_lab.py returns one of placement, no_move, or unknown.
Each invocation receives a supplied prior position and expected color. It does
not update a game, resolve captures, infer multiple moves, or generate presses.
Tools/evaluate_placement_competition.py runs the captured-data experiment.
Tools/placement_competition_protocol.json records its frozen numeric settings.

At each previously empty intersection with a raw stone candidate, the prototype
combines the classifier's heuristic confidence with optional appearance support.
Appearance support measures directional median change at the stone center after
adjusting the trusted reference using illumination measured in nearby wood.
All possible stone bodies are excluded from that wood measurement, and board
edges use the available one-sided samples. The current frame is compared with a
trusted earlier image, never a future clean image. Missing or unreliable wood
coverage is neutral; it cannot become evidence that the point is empty.

The relevant distinction in the captured incident is substantial: the real
Black stone has roughly 0.33–0.36 center darkening beyond the local wood change,
whereas the two false dark spots have roughly 0.03. These are grayscale feature
differences, not calibrated probabilities. White stones can also have low
contrast against pale wood, so low contrast alone cannot reject White.

Five controls
- raw_competition: raw-confidence support and temporal evidence, with competition.
- appearance_independent: appearance discount for both colors; one qualifying
  candidate may be selected without normalizing against weaker alternatives.
- appearance_competition: the same appearance evidence with location competition.
- black_shadow_independent: apply the appearance discount to Black only; keep
  White's existing raw-confidence evidence. No cross-location normalization.
- black_shadow_competition: the same color-specific evidence with competition.

Competition assigns relative support among expected-color locations and a null
alternative. A strong candidate reduces weaker candidates' shares, but does not
delete their observations. A winner must also have independently strong evidence
in two clear observations at least 0.12 seconds apart. A large null share alone
does not prove no move occurred: no_move requires repeated positive unchanged
appearance and confident empty readings throughout the relevant board area.
Otherwise the result is unknown. Shares are not posterior probabilities.

Hand obstruction/unavailable tracking, failed lighting, changed retained stones
and opposite-color additions force unknown. Possible captures remain for the
existing legal resolver. A camera gap over 0.5 seconds latches review until a new
trusted context is explicitly supplied; the model cannot silently recover a
single-turn assumption after an arbitrary gap. This conservative behavior is
not proposed as a replacement for the application's existing gap recovery.

Candidate history and timing
Every location/color pair preserves its own first observed time and observation
history, including weak and hand-obstructed sightings. These are not placement
times. Current independent support has its own consecutive witness run; a weak,
absent, obstructed or interrupted candidate breaks that run. A later appearance
cannot borrow an old supported timestamp. First-ever support remains archived.
Decision rows expose current-run firstSupported separately from confirmation.
Candidate rows also retain firstSupportedEver; history files preserve the full
first-ever evidence. Later hands do not retroactively erase good earlier images.

Original native frame timing estimates are copied as unassigned metadata. The
experiment never assigns a global frame estimate to one candidate as a clock
press. All decisions explicitly have clockAuthority=false. A first sighting
during a hand interaction must not be confused with a supported clock event.

Data and limits
The real rapid-reply segment has 75 analyzed views, with supplied conditional
contexts for White8, Black9 and White10. A separate Black-next probe starts with
the manually supplied position after White10, exposing the phantom Black at
raw point 323 (B19 in the rotated UI). Supplying this context does not claim
that the experiment automatically inferred White10 or decoded the whole game.
Three correct conditional placements are not three validated clock presses.

There are twelve delivery scenarios: recorded; the ten previous missed-hand,
unknown-hand, delayed-hand and camera-gap injections; and a negative control
removing precisely Black's frames 355/356. Original frame times are retained.
The 40-second quiet continuation is also replayed using its preserved unadapted
native readings. Rapid reply uses the earlier rerun pixel-classifier readings;
both sources are preserved and their distinction is explicit.

Twenty-two other captured photos supply 271 conditional stone/photo probes.
Each probe supplies all other actual stones as known context and asks about one
missing stone. This is a synthetic prior-position test, not recognition of a
whole board from nothing. Each photograph is held for five synthetic seconds;
repeated images add persistence, not independent visual evidence or real timing.
Known-position/no-additional-move probes supplement the placement probes.
Physical prisoners awaiting removal retain their existing physical labels.

All captures and labels were examined in previous work. This is development
evidence, not a fresh holdout. The earlier captured B19 false reading is not an
image of the much later saved false move 11; that later event lacks captured
images. Reviewed-frame scores and location-level episode results are reported
separately: a selection may occur between manually reviewed frames.

Results and iteration
Version 1 applied the same contrast discount to both colors. It avoided B19 and
retained the three real incident locations, but photo probe recognition dropped
from 230/271 in the raw control to 164/271. All 66 additional losses were White.
Version 2 therefore added the two Black-only controls. They recovered every one
of those 66 probes, still avoided B19, and retained the same real locations.
Version 3 hardens current-run timestamps against vanished/reappearing candidates.
Numeric thresholds did not change across these versions. Earlier results and
source snapshots are retained outside the repository. The color-specific choice
was informed by development outcomes; it is not an independently tested choice.

Raw competition selects B19 in the recorded Black-next probe and eight of its
repeated delivery contexts. Both Black-only controls select no incorrect
locations in those contexts. They recognize the same 230 photo probes as the
raw control, with no additional losses, wrong locations or false no-move results.
The remaining 41 unrecognized photo probes are already absent from this raw
experimental control; these counts are not native-classifier accuracy numbers.

Recorded conditional selection frames remain White8=335, Black9=356, White10=371.
Black's independent witnesses are the original frames 355/356 and their exact
capture times. The negative control without those witnesses abstains. White10
retains support beginning at 367, while opposite-color shadows delay selection
until 371; the experiment does not silently disregard that safeguard.

The competitive and independent versions make identical decisions on all 1,456
captured replay views compared for each appearance family, and have identical
photo-probe outcomes. Synthetic unit tests verify that competition functions,
including a case where extra weak candidates make it more conservative. There
is no measured reason yet to prefer the more complex competitive decision rule.

Remaining limitations are material. The no-move test returns unknown at all
eight reviewed quiet frames; even the first quiet view has 98 raw empty readings
below the conservative confidence floor. Thus no-move recognition is not yet
useful enough here. Missed-hand injections also expose 16 repeated selections
using witnesses that the original recording marked hand-obstructed, in both raw
and new controls. This is not 16 independent incidents. It demonstrates why
candidate appearance times cannot become clock presses without the existing
native timing safeguards. No clock-accuracy or pause-reduction claim is made.

Verification and next step
33 offline tests pass, covering competition/ties, no-move versus unknown,
unknown coverage, candidate-specific times, stale-timestamp isolation, late
hands, camera gaps, exposure failures, capture/opposite-color contradictions,
color-specific shadow support, and the earlier experiment regressions.
Source and observation fingerprints verify that live Swift files, raw readings,
captured times and pixel evidence remain unchanged. The app was not rebuilt,
installed or relaunched. No saved game or clock state was edited.

Freeze this candidate and collect a short fresh session containing real Black
edge placements, hand shadows with no placement, low-contrast White stones,
rapid replies and captures. Evaluate offline before any integration. Prioritize
validating the color-specific appearance evidence; competition has not earned
promotion. Native legal/capture/clock replay remains required before using any
new evidence in production decisions.
