Comparison and calibration

The frozen v3 support calculations were not retuned. At the existing 0.65
independent-support threshold, 65/65 reviewed post-placement points retain
correct strong support under both native and Black-shadow methods. This is a
single-frame support check, not the multi-frame placement/competition replay.
Capture handling is represented by labeled presence/removal samples, not an
assertion that this tool resolves captures.

Two exploratory ridge-logistic mappings estimate:
    P(the raw predicted stone color is correct | measured evidence).
This is not the probability a move happened, a no-move probability, a sequence
confidence, or a probability that a clock press time is correct.

Control features: intercept, native confidence, Black-vs-White indicator.
Joint features add raw-minus-shadow support and appearance-unavailable flag.
Ridge penalty is fixed at 1, excluding the intercept; no hyperparameter search.
Training uses only previously reviewed older photos and rapid-reply frames.
Initial occupied reference points are excluded from rapid-frame training,
because unchanged existing stones cannot supply empty-to-stone contrast.
Synthetic photo repetitions are deduplicated. Session/point/prediction/truth
groups each have total weight one to reduce repeated-frame influence.

There are 298 older stone readings in 71 weighted groups, with two errors,
BOTH from the same older rapid-reply session. Fresh labels never enter fitting.
Fresh images were inspected while developing the audit, so this is an inspected
audit set, not a pristine blinded validation set. Another session is needed
before any deployment claim. The sample deliberately includes a disagreement
and successful placements, so its proportions are not operational error rates.

Fresh scoring has 77 raw stone readings in 66 weighted groups, with one error.
The 57 empty readings remain in the audit but do not fit a stone-correctness
mapping. Episode weighting is not a claim of statistical independence.

Native scores are heuristics; treating them as probabilities here is diagnostic.
The joint model improves on fitting data but is slightly worse than the simpler
mapping on fresh data. Its estimate for the fresh false stone is still 0.9717,
versus 0.9636 for the simpler mapping. Neither reliably rejects this error.
The tiny overall differences, sparse errors, and inspected sample do not justify
promotion. No model or threshold is selected for live use.

This also answers the motivating question: verified correct low-score examples
can expose underconfidence, while verified wrong high-score examples expose
overconfidence. In the older sample, all 22 stone readings scoring 0.75–0.90
are correct, yet two readings scoring 1.0 are wrong. A scalar confidence score
alone cannot recover information it discarded. Two systems help when their
disagreement predicts correctness on independent data; merely choosing the
higher score or multiplying correlated scores does not establish that.
